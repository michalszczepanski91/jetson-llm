Reading one request

TTFT, tokens/sec and the "32-token turn" are three different measurements of the same round trip — not three unrelated numbers.

TTFT
Time to first token. Mostly prompt processing — what a user feels as "how long before it starts answering."
tok/s
Decode throughput once generation is underway. 1 ÷ tok/s is the average time per token.
32-token turn
Full wall-clock time for one realistic reply or tool call — the number this lab actually optimizes for.

What "per token" costs

Energy per token isn't total board draw — it's what generation costs above what the board burns just sitting there.

(board power while generating−idle power)÷tokens / sec=joules per token

Reading the sweep

No precision wins everything — but for a turn-latency-bound orchestrator, one is the clear pick.

INT4-GPTQ — recommended

2.5× the throughput, 2.5× shorter turns and a third of the energy of FP16, for only 4 points of escalation judgment. Wins tok/s, turn time and energy outright.

FP8 — near-lossless

Matches FP16 on accuracy almost exactly and edges out even INT4-GPTQ on time-to-first-token — but mid-pack on throughput, ~1.4× FP16 against INT4's 2.5×.

INT8-SQ — still open

Tool-calling intact, but MMLU sits 13.5 points below FP16 even after fixing an under-calibration bug. Not yet trusted for production.

Measured on an exclusive Thor with no other GPU workload; every latency figure reached thermal steady state. Board power is the whole board's 5 V input rail, not GPU-only. FP8 and INT8-SQ have no published checkpoint for this model and were quantized in-house with TensorRT Edge-LLM's own calibration tool — full methodology in docs/thor-framework-comparison.md.
